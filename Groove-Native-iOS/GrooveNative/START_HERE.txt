GROOVE NATIVE APP — FULL WALKTHROUGH
=========================================

This turns Groove into a real iPhone app with actual background/lock-
screen audio (the thing the web version can't do on iOS). No Mac needed.

You're doing 3 things: (1) upload this folder to GitHub, (2) let GitHub's
free cloud Mac build it, (3) install the result on your phone using a
free Windows tool.

Total time: maybe 20-30 minutes, most of it waiting for the build.


PART 1 — GET THIS ONTO GITHUB
----------------------------------
1. Go to github.com and sign up (free) if you don't have an account.
2. Click the "+" in the top right → "New repository".
3. Name it "groove-native" (or anything you want). Keep it Public.
   Don't check any of the boxes (no README, no .gitignore). Click
   "Create repository".
4. You'll land on a page with a box that says "uploading an existing
   file" — click that link.
5. Drag this ENTIRE folder's contents into that upload box — all of it:
   the "Sources" folder, "WebAssets" folder, "Assets.xcassets" folder,
   the ".github" folder, and "project.yml".

   IMPORTANT: GitHub's drag-and-drop upload page sometimes doesn't
   handle folders well. If dragging the whole thing doesn't work:
     - Open the folder on your computer
     - Select everything inside it (Ctrl+A)
     - Drag those selected items (not the outer folder itself) into
       the GitHub upload box
   The end result needs to be that "Sources", "WebAssets", etc. show up
   as folders sitting directly in your repo — not nested inside another
   folder called "GrooveNative".

6. Scroll down, click "Commit changes".


PART 2 — LET IT BUILD
--------------------------
1. In your new repo, click the "Actions" tab near the top.
2. You should see "Build Groove iOS App" listed. Click it.
3. Click "Run workflow" (dropdown button on the right) → "Run workflow"
   again to confirm.
4. Wait. Refresh after a minute — you'll see a run appear with a yellow
   dot (in progress), then either a green check (success) or red X
   (failed). This usually takes 3-8 minutes.

IF IT FAILS (red X):
   Click into the failed run, click the "build" job, and you'll see
   exactly which step failed and why in red text. Copy that error
   message and send it to me — I'll fix the config and you re-run it.
   This is normal for a first build; don't panic if it fails once.

IF IT SUCCEEDS (green check):
   Click into the run. Near the bottom, under "Artifacts", you'll see
   "Groove-ipa". Click it to download a zip — inside is Groove.ipa.


PART 3 — INSTALL ON YOUR IPHONE
------------------------------------
1. On your Windows PC, download Sideloadly: https://sideloadly.io
   (it's free — get the Windows version)
2. Install and open it.
3. Plug your iPhone into your PC with a cable. Trust the computer if
   your phone asks.
4. In Sideloadly, drag your Groove.ipa file into the window (or click
   the icon area to browse to it).
5. Enter your Apple ID email in the box (this is the free account you
   already use for the App Store — not a new signup, and Sideloadly
   doesn't need your password stored anywhere beyond signing the app).
6. Click "Start".
7. It'll ask for your Apple ID password once during signing — this is
   normal and required by Apple for any app installed this way.
8. Wait for it to finish. Groove will appear on your phone.

ONE MORE STEP ON YOUR PHONE (Apple requires this for any app installed
outside the App Store, every time, no way around it):
   Settings → General → VPN & Device Management → find your Apple ID
   under "Developer App" → tap it → "Trust".

Now open Groove from your Home Screen. Add your music the same way as
before — your old web-app library won't carry over automatically since
this is a separate app storage-wise, so you'll re-add your files once,
then they're saved permanently in this app too.


THE ONE CATCH WITH FREE APPLE IDS
--------------------------------------
Apps installed this way (without paying Apple's $99/year developer fee)
stop working after 7 days and need to be re-signed. Sideloadly has an
"anti-revoke" / auto-refresh feature for exactly this, or you can just
re-run steps 3-8 of Part 3 every week (quick once you've done it once).

If this becomes annoying, paying the $99/year removes the 7-day limit
entirely — that's the only difference the paid tier makes for personal
use like this.


WHY THIS ACTUALLY FIXES BACKGROUND AUDIO
---------------------------------------------
The Swift code in Sources/ContentView.swift sets up a real
AVAudioSession with the .playback category, and project.yml declares
the UIBackgroundModes "audio" capability in the app's Info.plist. Those
two things together are the actual Apple-only permission that lets an
app keep playing sound when the screen locks or you switch apps — it's
a capability that literally does not exist for websites, no matter how
they're built or installed. Everything else (the interface, your
library, EQ, playlists, themes) is the exact same code as the web
version, just running inside this wrapper instead of Safari.
